### 5. **“Linux 5.10 Reached EOL Today. Long-Term Support Is Still Finite.”** — Short / medium article


Seven stable Linux releases landed today, from **7.1.11 down through 5.10.267**, and the 5.10 branch has reached the end of its upstream long-term maintenance life. Today's updates also include fixes across NFC, Hyper-V guests, XFS, HID, and other subsystems. 

**Angle:** Linux 5.10 appeared in December 2020. Six years sounds enormous until you remember how long appliances survive.

There will be:

`routers`

`NAS boxes`

`industrial controllers`

`Android-derived devices`

`embedded boards`

still running it well into the 2030s.

This exposes the distinction between:

`product lifetime`

and:

`dependency support lifetime`.

Hardware vendors often implicitly promise the former without funding the latter.

A useful purchasing/design question for any embedded Linux product is therefore:

> **Who owns the security maintenance delta after upstream stops?**

Possible answers include the vendor, a commercial kernel-maintenance provider, your engineering team, or nobody.

“Nobody” is extremely common but rarely appears on the spec sheet.
