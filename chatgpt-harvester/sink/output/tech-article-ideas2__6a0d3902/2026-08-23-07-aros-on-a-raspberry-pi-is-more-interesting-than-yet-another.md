### 7. **“AROS on a Raspberry Pi Is More Interesting Than Yet Another Linux Distribution”** — Short post / retrocomputing article


Today's Hackaday coverage highlights **AROS running bare-metal on Raspberry Pi**, continuing the Amiga-compatible operating-system project's move onto contemporary ARM hardware. 

**Angle:** The interesting thing about alternative operating systems isn't whether they can replace Linux.

They preserve different answers to questions modern Unix-like systems have mostly standardised:

`What is a process?`

`How should IPC work?`

`What belongs in the OS?`

`How quickly should a machine boot?`

`How much abstraction is enough?`

Running an old architectural lineage on cheap modern hardware separates **historical hardware limitations from historical software-design choices**.

Some ideas turn out to have been clever because machines were slow.

Others turn out to remain clever when the machine is 10,000× faster.

Retrocomputing is useful partly because it provides a laboratory full of **alternative evolutionary branches of computing**.
