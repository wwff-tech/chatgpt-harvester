### 9. Left-field — “Maybe the Best Security Feature Is Having a Bigger Neighbour”


The PQC studies suggest something broader than cryptography: organisations behind large infrastructure providers inherit security improvements they neither requested nor necessarily understand. 

**Angle:** Explore **security inheritance**.

A small website behind a major CDN can inherit DDoS protection, TLS 1.3, PQ key exchange, bot mitigation, certificate automation, IPv6, HTTP/3, and patches to entire classes of protocol vulnerability.

Centralisation therefore creates an uncomfortable security trade-off:

**concentration increases systemic blast radius while simultaneously raising the security floor.**

The decentralised Internet may be architecturally beautiful, but millions of independently maintained Postfix, Apache, BIND, and OpenSSL installations aren't automatically a security win.

That's a nuanced argument worth making rather than another “centralisation bad” piece.
