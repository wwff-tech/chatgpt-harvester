### 10. **Left-field — “RS-232 May Outlive Your REST API”** — Long-form / short essay


The Moxa driver is an excuse for a fun piece about technological longevity. 

**Angle:** Why do some interfaces live for sixty years while others barely survive six?

Long-lived interfaces tend to be:

`simple`

`loosely coupled`

`fully implementable`

`vendor-independent`

`locally understandable`

`usable without infrastructure`.

RS-232 requires two endpoints and some wire.

A modern cloud API may depend on:

`vendor`

`DNS`

`TLS PKI`

`OAuth provider`

`Internet`

`billing relationship`

`current SDK`

`service still existing`.

The newer system is enormously more capable, but its **dependency surface is also vastly larger**.

This isn't an argument for going back to serial ports.

It's an argument that *simplicity is one of the mechanisms by which technology acquires longevity*.
