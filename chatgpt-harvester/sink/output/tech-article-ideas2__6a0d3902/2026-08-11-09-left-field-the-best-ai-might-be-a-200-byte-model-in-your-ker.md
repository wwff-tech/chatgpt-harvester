### 9. Left-field — “The Best AI Might Be a 200-Byte Model in Your Kernel”


LearnedCache suggests an enjoyable counterpoint to the current definition of AI. 

**Angle:** We've culturally conflated “AI” with enormous generative models, despite many infrastructure problems being narrow optimisation tasks with plentiful ground truth.

If you're deciding whether a page is likely to be reused, you don't need Shakespeare, Python, visual reasoning, and knowledge of 18th-century Prussian history.

You need something that answers:

> `reuse(page, workload_state) → probability`

cheaply and reliably.

That opens a broader essay about **right-sizing intelligence**:

- linear models for control,
- decision trees for policy,
- anomaly models for telemetry,
- small classifiers for routing,
- LLMs only where semantic ambiguity genuinely requires them.

The analogy writes itself: putting an LLM into every control loop is rather like replacing every microcontroller with a Threadripper because Threadrippers benchmark better.
