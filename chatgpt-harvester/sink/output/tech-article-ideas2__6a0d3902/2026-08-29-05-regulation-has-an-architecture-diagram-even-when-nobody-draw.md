### 5. Regulation Has an Architecture Diagram, Even When Nobody Draws It


AB 1856 gives a good general lesson. 

**Angle:** Technical regulation frequently contains invisible assumptions such as:

`there is one vendor`

`the vendor has accounts`

`the vendor controls updates`

`software comes from one store`

`the device is permanently online`

`identity maps cleanly to a human`.

Those aren't policy decisions.

They're **system architecture assumptions**.

If the law only works when those assumptions hold, architectures outside the assumed model either become impossible, accidentally non-compliant, or need exemptions.

A worthwhile test for technology legislation is therefore:

> **Draw the architecture the law assumes exists.**

Then try Debian, F-Droid, self-hosting, offline systems, corporate-managed devices, shared family computers, and embedded appliances against it.

You'll probably find the edge cases before the courts do.
