### 6. **“Pine64 Has Hit Pause Because RAM Became the Expensive Part”** — Long-form / hardware post


PINE64 announced today that it is **pausing manufacture of Linux devices** rather than simply increasing prices, citing current RAM and eMMC shortages and elevated component costs. 

**Angle:** This is much more interesting than a supply-chain update.

For years, cheap ARM hardware has benefited from smartphone-scale component economics. SBC makers don't control DRAM or flash markets; they surf much larger supply chains.

That creates an uncomfortable property:

> **Open hardware can be architecturally independent while remaining economically dependent on closed mass-market ecosystems.**

A board schematic may be open. Linux may be open. The bootloader may be open.

But if Samsung, Micron, SK hynix, etc. move memory economics somewhere inconvenient, your £50 Linux computer stops existing.

There is a broader resilience argument here about BOM design, interchangeable components, soldered eMMC versus removable storage, long-lived industrial SKUs, and designing hardware around commodity parts whose commodity status can disappear.
