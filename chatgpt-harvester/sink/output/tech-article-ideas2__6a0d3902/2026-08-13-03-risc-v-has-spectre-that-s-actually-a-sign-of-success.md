### 3. RISC-V Has Spectre. That's Actually a Sign of Success.


Researchers from CISPA Helmholtz Center for Information Security and KU Leuven have demonstrated Spectre-family speculative-execution attacks against **commercial RISC-V processors**. Reporting on the work appeared today. 

**Angle:** Don't frame this as “RISC-V security failure”.

It's almost the opposite.

RISC-V implementations are becoming sufficiently sophisticated to acquire the same class of microarchitectural optimisation problems as high-performance x86 and ARM processors.

The distinction worth explaining is:

`ISA specification ≠ microarchitecture`

An open instruction set doesn't prescribe branch predictors, speculative execution, caches, prefetchers, or the many other implementation choices that create side channels.

This is also a useful antidote to the simplistic idea that open hardware is automatically secure. Openness makes inspection and correction *possible*; it doesn't abolish physics or implementation bugs.
