### 10. Left-field — “The Cheapest Computer Is Usually Built by Somebody Else's Industry”


Pine64's pause exposes something broader about commodity computing. 

**Angle:** Raspberry Pi, cheap SBCs, ESP32 boards, webcams, routers, and tiny PCs are affordable partly because their expensive components exist for **much larger adjacent markets**.

Hobbyists don't fund DRAM fabs.

Home automation doesn't fund Wi-Fi chipset development.

Retrocomputing doesn't fund LCD factories.

We inherit economies of scale from phones, laptops, televisions, automotive electronics, and hyperscalers.

This means entire maker ecosystems can shift when the *parent market* changes direction.

A lovely example is small LCDs: once a particular phone-panel size becomes obsolete, a formerly cheap display can become strangely expensive or disappear entirely.

The broader principle:

> **Commodity is not an intrinsic property of a component. It is a temporary consequence of somebody else's volume.**
