### 9. Left-field — “A Bug Report Is a Request to Spend Someone Else's Time”


The QEMU flood suggests a broader economic framing. 

**Angle:** Many Internet systems price only the sender's resource consumption.

Sending:

`email`

`issue`

`PR`

`support ticket`

`security report`

costs almost nothing.

But the recipient pays:

`triage`

`context switching`

`verification`

`response`

`maintenance`.

LLMs radically reduce the sender-side cost without reducing the receiver-side cost.

So abuse emerges even without malicious intent.

This is a **cost asymmetry attack**:

\[
C_{sender} \ll C_{receiver}
\]

Spam has always worked this way. AI is bringing spam economics into engineering collaboration.

That suggests future collaboration systems will increasingly need mechanisms that make the submitter provide **cheaply verifiable evidence of expensive work**.
