### 7. **“Small Models Keep Winning When the Harness Does the Thinking”** — Long-form / watch item


London-based Inherent says its Faraday research agent outperformed agents based on much larger frontier models at reproducing published scientific results while using a **27-billion-parameter Qwen 3.6 model** as its core. Faraday also delegates coding work to a specialised coding system rather than attempting everything through one model. These are company-reported results, so I'd treat the benchmark claims cautiously. 

**Angle:** The interesting claim isn't “27B beats GPT-5.5”.

It's another piece of evidence for:

`system capability ≠ model capability`.

An agent system has at least:

`model`

`tools`

`search strategy`

`environment`

`memory/state`

`verification`

`specialist delegation`

`training/reward`.

Increasing only model size is therefore analogous to improving a database-backed web service by **buying a faster CPU before looking at the query plan**.

The fact that Faraday delegates coding is particularly interesting. A general research agent doesn't need to *be* the best coder if it knows when to call one.

That looks much more like good engineering organisations:

`orchestrator/generalist → specialist → evidence → integration`.

**Matches:** small-model orchestration, specialist agents, research agents, tool use, agent architecture.

**Format:** **Long-form/watch item**, preferably after independent evaluation appears.

**Confidence: 0.75 on comparative performance; 0.96 on the architectural argument.**
