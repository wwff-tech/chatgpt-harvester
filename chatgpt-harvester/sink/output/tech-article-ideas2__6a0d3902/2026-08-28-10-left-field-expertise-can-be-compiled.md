### 10. Left-field — “Expertise Can Be Compiled”


The QuEra result points at something broader than AI or quantum computing. 

**Angle:** Expert operators frequently possess tacit knowledge:

> If it oscillates like *that*, back this parameter off, wait, then approach from below.

Traditional automation struggles because nobody has completely specified the procedure.

AI potentially provides a bridge:

`expert examples + experimentation`

→ `search through behaviour`

→ `discover successful strategy`

→ `compile strategy into conventional controller`.

Once compiled, the organisation no longer needs an expert present for every occurrence.

That is qualitatively different from “replace the expert with an AI chatbot”.

The AI becomes a **knowledge compiler**, transforming expensive tacit judgement into cheap repeatable machinery.

The interesting organisational consequence is that the most valuable automation targets may not be high-volume clerical tasks. They may be **rare, expensive interventions currently bottlenecked on a handful of specialists**.

Think:

`weird database recovery`

`RF calibration`

`production tuning`

`instrument alignment`

`network fault diagnosis`

`hardware bring-up`.
