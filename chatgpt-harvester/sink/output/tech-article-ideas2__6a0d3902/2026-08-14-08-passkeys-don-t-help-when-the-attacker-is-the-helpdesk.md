### 8. Passkeys Don't Help When the Attacker Is the Helpdesk


Fresh Google Threat Intelligence reporting on UNC6671 describes voice-phishing campaigns where attackers impersonate IT staff conducting urgent security migrations, contact employees on personal mobiles, and direct them to adversary-in-the-middle credential portals. Once sessions are captured, automation exfiltrates data from SaaS environments including Microsoft 365 and Okta. 

**Angle:** The interesting lesson isn't “users remain the weakest link”.

Attackers increasingly target **security ceremonies** themselves:

> “We're upgrading MFA.”

> “You need to enrol your passkey.”

> “We're migrating SSO.”

A user who has been trained that security changes are important may actually become *more compliant* with a convincing fake migration.

So secure authentication rollout needs a **trusted communication path about changes to the trusted communication path**.

That's a wonderfully recursive security problem.
