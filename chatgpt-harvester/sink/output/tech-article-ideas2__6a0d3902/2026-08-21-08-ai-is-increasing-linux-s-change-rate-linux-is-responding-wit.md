### 8. **“AI Is Increasing Linux's Change Rate. Linux Is Responding With Conservatism, Not AI Governance”** — Long-form / watch item


In the Linux 7.2 release announcement, Linus Torvalds described another larger-than-desired final week as part of the current “new normal”. Recent reporting connects the increased contribution volume partly to AI-assisted development. Yet the practical response visible in 7.2 is remarkably traditional: review, regression reports, late reverts, and refusal to ship a problematic new default. 

**Angle:** This complements the Debian discussion from last week.

Perhaps the scalable response to cheap code generation isn't primarily:

`detect AI code`

but:

`make contribution cheap`

**while keeping acceptance expensive enough to mean something.**

Linux doesn't ultimately trust the author, human or machine. It trusts a layered process of maintainership, testing, subsystem ownership, regression reports, release candidates, and willingness to revert.

That's another example of the distinction between **generation throughput and acceptance throughput**.

**Matches:** Linux, open-source governance, AI-assisted coding, software assurance.

**Format:** **Long-form**, although I'd probably wait for another cycle before treating the “new normal” as established evidence.
