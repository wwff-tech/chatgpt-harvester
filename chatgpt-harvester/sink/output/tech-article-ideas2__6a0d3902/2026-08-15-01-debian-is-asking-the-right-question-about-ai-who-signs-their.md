### 1. Debian Is Asking the Right Question About AI: Who Signs Their Name to the Code?


Debian developers have moved into voting on their General Resolution covering LLM use. The choices span outright prohibition through conditional acceptance, but the permissive proposals contain unusually concrete requirements: contributors remain accountable, must understand and defend submitted work, must verify licensing, should disclose significant AI assistance, must discuss bulk automated changes beforehand, and must not expose confidential project material to untrusted models. 

**Angle:** Ignore the culture-war framing of “AI: yes or no?” The much better engineering question is:

> **What does authorship mean when generation is cheap but accountability cannot be automated?**

Debian's emerging model is essentially *trust the contributor, not the generator*. A human signature is an assertion that someone has performed sufficient verification to take responsibility for the result.

That generalises beautifully to coding agents, CI-generated changes, dependency bots, infrastructure agents, and automated remediation.

`machine proposes → human understands → tests verify → human attests → project accepts`

This is far more useful than pretending “human-written” and “AI-written” are reliably distinguishable categories.
