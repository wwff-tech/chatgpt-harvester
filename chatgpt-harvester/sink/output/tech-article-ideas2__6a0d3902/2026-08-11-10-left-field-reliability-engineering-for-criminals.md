### 10. Left-field — “Reliability Engineering for Criminals”


DeadLock exposes a slightly uncomfortable teaching device: adversarial infrastructure strips reliability engineering down to its economic fundamentals. 

**Angle:** Imagine reviewing its architecture without being told what it does.

You might praise:

- removal of central dependencies,
- encrypted communications,
- dynamic endpoint discovery,
- provider independence,
- graceful infrastructure replacement,
- censorship resistance,
- minimal operator identity leakage.

Then reveal that you've just reviewed ransomware infrastructure.

The lesson isn't merely that technology is dual-use. It's that **architecture is an optimisation function**. Change the objective function and exactly the same engineering principles optimise something socially destructive.

That's a useful route into why “best practice” never exists independently of threat model, incentives, and desired outcomes.

**Matches:** SRE, systems architecture, threat modelling, engineering ethics, decentralised systems.
