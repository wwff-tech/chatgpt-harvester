### 2. The Human Didn't Leave the Loop. The Loop Got Bigger.


The Taiwan reporting also undercuts one of the least useful AI-security dichotomies: *autonomous versus human-operated*. Cybersecurity experts quoted in current coverage still describe humans setting objectives and directing AI tooling even where substantial attack stages are automated. 

**Angle:** Human-in-the-loop is often treated as a binary safety property.

It isn't.

One person manually executing 20 actions is very different from:

`1 human → 20 agents → 400 tools → 20,000 actions`

while technically remaining “human supervised”.

That suggests a more useful metric: **human leverage ratio**.

How much consequential machine activity can one human authorise, supervise, or cause per unit time?

This applies equally to defensive agents, coding agents, infrastructure automation, drones, financial agents, and cyber offence.
