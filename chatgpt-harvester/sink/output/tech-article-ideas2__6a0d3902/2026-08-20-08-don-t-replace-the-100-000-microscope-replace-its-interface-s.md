### 8. **“Don't Replace the £100,000 Microscope. Replace Its Interface.”** — Short post / maker article


A project receiving coverage today, **Open Beam Interface**, adds digital image acquisition to compatible legacy scanning electron microscopes by intercepting detector and scan signals rather than replacing the microscope's core systems. 

**Angle:** This is a lovely example of **interface modernisation instead of equipment replacement**.

Engineering equipment often has:

`excellent physics`

`excellent mechanics`

`excellent analogue electronics`

`awful 1997 computer`.

The commercially encouraged answer is often replacing the whole instrument.

The hacker answer is:

`retain expensive physical capability`

`replace obsolete digital boundary`.

This generalises to oscilloscopes, CNC machines, environmental chambers, laboratory equipment, industrial controllers, telescopes, and test gear.

It also overlaps nicely with the KVM story from Tuesday: **the valuable upgrade is often making old physical capability machine-readable.**
