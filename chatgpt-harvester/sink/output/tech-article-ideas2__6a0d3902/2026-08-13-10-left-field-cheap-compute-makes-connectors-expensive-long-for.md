### 10. **Left-field — “Cheap Compute Makes Connectors Expensive”** — Long-form / essay seed


The WCH MCU is another example of a decades-long hardware trend: compute and memory keep collapsing towards negligible marginal cost while **interfaces to the physical world stubbornly don't**. 

**Angle:** A surprisingly large modern electronics BOM increasingly consists of things silicon cannot cheaply abstract away:

- connectors,
- isolation,
- magnetics,
- antennas,
- relays,
- power conversion,
- protection,
- sensors,
- mechanical assembly.

A 200 MHz computer may cost less than a decent locking connector attached to it.

There's a broader engineering lesson here: **Moore's Law makes the non-scaling parts of systems progressively more important**.

Software has an analogue too. Compute became cheap; coordination, verification, human attention, and integration didn't.
